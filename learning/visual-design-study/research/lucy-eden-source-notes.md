## Three concrete examples from her own studio

### Michelle Elman: unity and restrained contextual detail

Her description identifies candid, empathetic, bold and wise personality traits, then describes a custom signature-style wordmark whose lettering nests into a unified arrangement. A speech-bubble detail sits over the “i”; supporting illustrations and patterns carry additional topics and personality.

**Transferable review question:** Do the letters read as one deliberate composition, and can richer context live in supporting elements instead of being forced into the core mark? This question is our interpretation of the example. It is not a quote or a claim of tested superiority.

Source opened: https://www.edenbrands.us/case-studies/michelle-elman

### Splatter: letter behavior tied to the experience

For a paint-throwing experience positioned around creative freedom, her account connects bold, edgy and playful personality to slanted, stacked custom lettering. A condensed variation adapts it into a symmetrical badge. She describes physically painting and scanning splashes to create original patterns and icons.

**Transferable review question:** Does the mark's structure express a specific aspect of the experience, and are its alternative configurations intentionally designed? Originality here includes how the supporting artwork was made, not just how unusual the headline looks.

Source opened: https://www.edenbrands.us/case-studies/splatter

### Marlowe & Sage: a serif can carry approachable personality

Her childcare-brand account identifies nurturing, warm, wise and encouraging traits. It describes rounded serif lettering and an intertwined ampersand; the smaller mark reuses initials and the ampersand relationship. Simple shapes, leaves and light illustration support the broader system.

**Transferable review question:** Is a distinctive device repeated coherently between the full name and small mark? This example also rejects a blanket “serif equals traditional and wrong” rule: the intended tone and the actual letter shapes matter.

Source opened: https://www.edenbrands.us/case-studies/marlowe-and-sage
