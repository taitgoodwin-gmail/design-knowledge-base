## 00 — Purpose
AugMind delivers disciplined advisory decisions for clients drowning in digital noise, AI hype, and half-baked agency work. We are not a marketing agency and not a software shop. The product is the decision system.
Three layers, in order:
1. **Three Frameworks** — how we think (structured listening, structured problem decomposition, Big Four advisory gates).
2. **Delivery Methodology** — how we work (five numbered stages, outputs, stop-points).
3. **Pricing Methodology** — how we charge (visible work, named people, recurring run).
---
## 01 — Three Frameworks (thinking layer)
### 1.1 Structured listening — conversation truth
Lock the need in the client's own words before any solution is offered. No tool names in the listening session. Capture current state, desired future state, and the gap between them.
### 1.2 Structured problem decomposition — analytical truth
Break the problem into current state, future state, and the decomposition of what must be true for the future state to hold. Kill bad explanations with evidence. Provisional answers only. Proposed budgets are not treated as results.
### 1.3 Big Four advisory gates — contractual truth
Baseline, success metric, scope, acceptance, change control. Clear stops. Discovery can end the work. Tactics are not "just optimized."
**Contrast sentence:** An agency standardizes the ads. We standardize the decision.
---
## 02 — Delivery Methodology (five stages)
Each stage has: a decision, named work, a minimum output, and a completion gate where the client can stop or choose next steps.
### 01 — Discover and recommend
- **Decision:** What is the real need, in their words?
- **Work:** Structured listening; current-state mapping; future-state definition; structured problem decomposition; evidence review against benchmarks.
- **Output:** D1.1 — Need statement + current/future state + decomposition + evidence map.
- **Gate:** G1 — Client confirms the need and accepts the decomposition, or names gaps.
### 02 — Design and plan *(measurement lock lives here)*
- **Decision:** What does good look like, and how will we know?
- **Work:** Success metric in client's words, data source, named owner, scope, staffing, pricing.
- **Output:** D2.1 — Plan with locked metric, source, owner; D2.2 — Single Statement of Work covering all five stages + acceptance criteria.
- **Gate:** G2 — Metric, source, and owner signed; scope and price accepted.
- **Rule:** No metric, no implementation. (Discovery can still run without one.)
- **Source check:** One master Statement of Work at engagement start is standard advisory practice (Deloitte engagement letters define the full scope; KPMG advisory Statements of Work reference a Master Services Agreement and cover the defined phases). Stages 03–05 execute inside it; changes go through change control, not new Statements of Work.
### 03 — Implement *(change control lives here)*
- **Decision:** Build or configure the agreed scope.
- **Work:** Execute plan; any scope move, dead hypothesis, or channel shift requires a written change note — what changed, why, evidence, who accepted.
- **Output:** D3.1 — Working deliverable + change log.
- **Gate:** G3 — Deliverable matches accepted scope, or change note approved.
### 04 — Validate and Run *(the business model)*
- **Decision:** Can the client accept the result, and is it ready to run under AugMind?
- **Work:** Check against acceptance criteria; transfer ownership and access; stand up the repeatable process — automation, monitoring, operating cadence.
- **Output:** D4.1 — Accepted deliverables + running process with named owners, support boundaries, recurring operating plan.
- **Gate:** G4 — Client accepts against criteria, or names specific gaps. Silence is not acceptance. Running starts only after acceptance.
### 05 — Review results and improve *(quality gate lives here)*
- **Decision:** Did it work, and what recurs?
- **Work:** Measure against locked metric; on higher-value or higher-risk work, one named person outside the delivery team reviews the final deliverable before it reaches the client (risk-based, like advisory practice — not every job).
- **Output:** D5.1 — Results review + improvement backlog + next-run plan.
- **Gate:** G5 — Results reviewed; next cycle scoped or closed.

## 03 — Pricing Methodology (pricing layer)
- **Doctrine:** Named people at published rates by level. Fixed scope = fixed number. Unclear scope = short paid diagnostic first, never an open meter. Tools inside the rate. Client-bought items pass through at cost.
- **Four ways to buy:** Diagnostic (credits in full against follow-on), Fixed-fee project, Range with a cap, Capacity.
- **Recurring tier:** Because we run, not hand off — monthly or quarterly retainer tied to the automated processes and operating cadence stood up in stage 04.
- **No rate discounts.** If the number must come down, scope shrinks.
- **Internal only:** Partners adopt the rate card before any Statement of Work ships. Client never sees "proposed."

## 04 — Quality system (advisory-grade, not audit-grade)
- Risk-based review, not mandatory independent review on every job.
- One named reviewer outside the delivery team signs off on higher-value or higher-risk deliverables.
- Change control is mandatory on every scope move (stage 03).
- Measurement lock is mandatory before implementation (stage 02).
- **Verified sources (advisory only):**
	- KPMG Integrated Report 2024/2025 (Netherlands): In Advisory, Quality Performance Reviews are performed by the Functional Quality & Risk Management Partner on two criteria — engagement set-up and engagement execution — rated green, yellow, or red. Both green and yellow are satisfactory. ([corporatereporting.kpmg.nl](http://corporatereporting.kpmg.nl))
	- Deloitte engagement letter change-control appendix (publicly filed, Post Office Horizon inquiry evidence): written Change Request, impact investigation, formal Change Order; nothing is effective until signed by authorized representatives of both parties. ([postofficeinquiry.dracos.co.uk](http://postofficeinquiry.dracos.co.uk))
	- KPMG advisory Statements of Work publicly filed in U.S. court dockets explicitly state the services constitute an Advisory Engagement under AICPA Standards for Consulting Services — not audit, examination, or attestation.
