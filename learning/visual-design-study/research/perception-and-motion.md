# Perception, material and motion: a design learning note

Prepared 7 September 2026. This is a learning resource, not a proposed website or brand identity. Inspected the supplied Organic Tech, Claymorphism, Dirty Gradients, Clean Girl Brutalism and overview JPEGs in `../references/`. They are low-resolution social-media captures with interface overlays, not source artwork or working websites. The style names are the video's labels, not universally agreed categories. Holographic Iridescence appears only as a small overview thumbnail.

Observed: Organic Tech places a clean white “Decode yourself” panel over porous purple and pink forms. Claymorphism combines brightly colored folded, rounded and glossy shapes behind white event text. Dirty Gradients shows six poster rectangles with broad horizontal color transitions and mixed type. Clean Girl Brutalism places a very heavy “AGNI” heading above a large quiet area and small pink information block. The holographic thumbnail suggests reflective, multicolored packaging, but is too small to identify its material or technique. These observations motivate the principles below; they do not verify readability or interaction.

## What makes these styles work

**Heavy editorial type and space.** Large letters can become the dominant shape of a composition: the reader encounters a statement before inspecting its supporting details. But hierarchy is relational. If the headline, navigation, caption and button all demand equal emphasis, increasing their weight does not establish a useful reading order. My design interpretation is to allocate one dominant typographic gesture, then support it with quieter, sufficiently legible information. Negative space is the interval that keeps those roles distinct; it can be colored or textured rather than literally white.

Cooper Hewitt's *How Posters Work* frames posters as deliberate experiments in perception and storytelling. Its account of Philippe Apeloig's *Vivo in Typo* documents typography constructed through punctuation, scale, spacing, drawing and print texture. These are useful precedents for studying how type can serve as image; neither source establishes that a particular font improves website conversion. [Museum exhibition](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/exhibitions/how-posters-work/); [documented making process](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2014/12/05/making-a-poster-is-a-process/).

**Organic tech and clay-like forms.** Rounded contours, broad soft highlights and grounding shadows can suggest volume and softness. “Friendly,” “futuristic” and “expensive” are interpretations, not physical properties or universal reactions. A shape with attractive rendering can still be irrelevant to the message. Ask what its form explains: a connection, transformation, sequence, container, or simply atmosphere. If it is atmosphere, do not give it the strongest visual emphasis by default.

Material appearance is not determined by a single color. Storrs, Anderson and Fleming's research examines the interaction of reflectance, lighting and geometry in perceived gloss, including misperceptions. That supports caution about simplistic recipes such as “add a highlight to make metal.” It does not prove a particular clay illustration feels reassuring or authentic. [Open research paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01097-6).

**Grainy or “dirty” gradients.** Here the useful distinction is between a slow change in color and small-scale surface variation. My interpretation is that grain can interrupt the perfectly smooth appearance associated with digital rendering and suggest print, pigment or photographic texture. Its effect depends on density, scale and placement: texture that is appealing in an empty field can interfere with thin text. The lesson is not “grain equals craft.” Craft includes choosing where the texture stops and protecting the reading surface. Apeloig's process is a relevant precedent for purposeful tactility, not evidence that digital noise reproduces screenprinting.

**Holographic or iridescent treatments.** In interface design these labels often describe a visual simulation: shifting bands of color, sharp highlights and alternating light/dark regions. A static multicolor gradient alone does not establish physical iridescence. For learning, distinguish hue variation from highlight structure and apparent depth. My interpretation is that the material effect becomes more convincing when color and lighting appear to belong to a coherent surface. The practical consequence is to separate decorative material rendering from text and control states. A button's identity should survive when the decorative sheen is removed.

## Grouping makes space meaningful

Gestalt principles offer explanations of relationships, not an automatic template. Palmer describes grouping by proximity, similarity, common region, connectedness and synchrony. A bounded field can make separated items read as one set; changes at the same time can also imply a relationship. [Researcher's own discussion](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/Palmer.pdf).

Applied interpretation: before adding boxes to every item, first adjust their relative distances. Put a caption visibly closer to its image than to the next image. Conversely, if independent actions sit inside one prominent organic shape, that shared region may imply they are one action. A decorative line joining unrelated elements can communicate a relationship the copy does not support. Motion has the same responsibility: synchronized movement can imply a common system even when the elements are independent. On a narrow screen, preserve the intended relationships when the layout changes; merely stacking everything can produce a new grouping.

## Translate the principle, not the poster dimensions

A poster has a fixed surface. A website must accommodate longer text, different viewport widths, zoom and interaction. Preserve its hierarchy, rhythm and material character while letting the composition change. A dramatic headline can wrap differently on a phone; a peripheral sculpture can become a smaller detail; a full-page texture can become an isolated image. These are design translations rather than evidence of aesthetic compromise.

Use editable text for meaningful headings and descriptions so it can reflow. W3C's Reflow criterion expects ordinary vertically scrolling content to work at a width equivalent to 320 CSS pixels without lost information or functionality or two-dimensional scrolling, subject to specified exceptions. That is a testable requirement, not proof that every single-column composition is usable. [W3C Reflow explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).

For gradients, contrast must be considered where the letters actually sit, including changing backgrounds. W3C specifies 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for qualifying large text, with exceptions. Thin or unusual fonts can remain visually faint despite nominally passing values. My recommendation is a controlled reading region rather than trusting a palette swatch sampled elsewhere. No screenshot in this study has received a contrast pass. [W3C Contrast explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

Motion can demonstrate a transformation or acknowledge an action, but a living background does not need to make reading itself move. W3C's Animation from Interactions criterion is Level AAA and addresses disabling nonessential interaction-triggered motion. Separately, Level A Pause, Stop, Hide covers qualifying automatically moving information lasting more than five seconds alongside other content. Reduced-motion support and a pause control answer related but different needs. [Interaction motion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html); [automatic movement](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html).

Rendering cost depends on implementation. Google's browser guidance recommends favoring transform and opacity where appropriate, and warns against indiscriminate `will-change`. This is not a guarantee that a large blurred animated layer will be cheap. For these material studies, compare a static rendered texture with the animated version on actual devices before accepting the added work. Record asset bytes and a performance trace, rather than declaring a design fast from its appearance. [Browser performance guidance](https://web.dev/articles/animations-and-performance).

## Three small teaching experiments

These are proposed experiments, not completed tests. All can run in one local HTML file with CSS, inline SVG and JavaScript, native labeled controls, and no network requests.

1. **Space establishes relationships.** Keep a heading, two image placeholders, two captions and a button identical. A range input changes only the gap between each image and its caption; maintain the outer canvas size. Ask which caption belongs to which image and what feels grouped. Show the current gap numerically and provide reset. Compare the same setting at wide and narrow widths separately. This demonstrates a spacing effect without simultaneously changing font or color.

2. **Texture versus reading.** Keep layout, type, text color and base gradient fixed. A slider changes only the opacity of one deterministic grain layer. Ask when the surface becomes more tactile and when small text becomes harder to inspect. Keep the grain seed fixed so settings are comparable. Label subjective responses as preferences; do not output an invented accessibility score. A separate solid-background reference helps explain why a palette's nominal contrast cannot describe every textured pixel.

3. **Still versus moving material.** Use an identical decorative form with a fixed iridescent-looking surface. Compare a still state with the same form translating through a small distance; do not also rotate, resize or recolor it. A user-activated Play/Pause button controls movement, and reduced-motion preference defaults it to still. Ask whether the motion explains anything or competes with the unchanged text. Keep the scene visible when paused. The experiment isolates motion; it does not claim to simulate optical iridescence accurately.

## Evidence limits and next learning step

Museum process accounts, perception research and accessibility guidance answer different questions. None identifies the user's preferred style or proves commercial success. Unknowns include the original references' authorship and production techniques, actual target fonts and assets, motion behavior, device performance, and audience comprehension. Some Nature and PMC pages were blocked during retrieval; only the successfully opened gloss paper is used here. Palmer's 1992 publisher page failed to open; the successfully opened 2002 paper from his lab supplies the grouping discussion. Build and inspect the three experiments before treating their expected effects as observed results.
