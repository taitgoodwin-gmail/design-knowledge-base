# Contemporary material aesthetics: research notes

Research date: 7 September 2026. Scope: Claymorphism, Organic Tech, Dirty Gradients, Spray, and Holographic Iridescence. This is an exploratory study, not a Levarum direction approval or a claim that any style improves conversion.

## Evidence and terminology

I visually inspected the supplied, unmodified screenshots `02-claymorphism.jpeg`, `03-organic-tech.jpeg`, `04-dirty-gradients.jpeg`, `07-spray.jpeg`, and `09-overview.jpeg`. Their trend labels are useful conversational handles, not mutually exclusive technical classifications. An object can be geometrically soft, iridescent in material, and surrounded by a grainy gradient simultaneously. The studios cited below do not necessarily classify their work under these labels.

I opened the primary pages linked below and read the retrieved content. I have not independently run their interactions or tested audience responses. Some separate embedded image requests failed; visual observations of the supplied screenshots are distinguished from creators' descriptions. The image showing “Simplicity21” led to a matching project title and named creators on an original portfolio; this supports project identification, but I have not performed a pixel-for-pixel match of its frame. Attribution of the other supplied crops remains unconfirmed.

## 1. Claymorphism: form and touch

**Observed in the reference:** the Simplicity21 crop contains oversized blue curved fins, coral cylinders, green folds, and orange rounded forms. Highlights and shadows communicate volume; softened edges and saturated colors create a tactile, toy-like impression. It is more varied than “pastel puffy UI cards.” Glossy and matte-looking surfaces coexist.

**Documented original project:** Kyungtae Kim and Toss Design's [Simplicity 21 portfolio](https://www.behance.net/gallery/127686975/Simplicity-21) credits Kim with art direction and 3D design for Viva Republica's conference. Five objects represent the conference title and four themes: Obsession, Details, Extra Mile, and Paradigm Shift. The imagery has a content system behind it; it is not simply a collection of fashionable blobs.

**Related primary case:** [Microsoft's Fluent emoji account](https://microsoft.design/articles/an-emoji-for-your-thoughts-2/) describes building expressive characters from simple geometry, choosing imperfect silhouettes, and preserving emotional intent. It documents a communication purpose for playful volume. It does not establish that clay-styled marketing pages outperform other pages.

**Interpretation:** tactile form can make abstract concepts approachable and group related ideas through consistent material and lighting. In a creative conference, exuberance can feel appropriate; on a critical transaction screen the same scale and density could distract. A rendered object used as illustration does not automatically have the affordances of an actual button. Keep functional controls visually and semantically clear.

## 2. Organic Tech: biological complexity against rational structure

**Observed:** the supplied “Decode yourself” composition surrounds a very plain white information card with porous, tufted and mesh-like purple/pink volumes. The effect comes from contrast: intricate material outside, sparse typographic information inside. “Organic” here does not require leaves, green, or literal environmental messaging.

**Documented adjacent case:** [Cartier's Farinart identity](https://agencecartier.com/en/projects/farinart-a-new-identity-crops-up/) deliberately combines innovation and agricultural origins. Its designers describe modeled 3D grains, landscape photography, geometric iconography and contrasting type. The visual subject is connected to the company's actual material, rather than borrowing generic nature symbolism.

**Interpretation:** this family can express adaptation, interdependence, emergence, or a connection between living systems and engineered systems. Scale changes the reading: a small fuzzy object may seem friendly; an enlarged porous surface can suggest microscopy, biotech, fungi or something unsettling. Clean technical typography can make those textures feel examined and purposeful. Without a relevant subject or explanation, the same imagery can imply a scientific capability that the business does not possess. Nature-like appearance is also not evidence of environmental benefit.

## 3. Dirty Gradients: atmosphere with irregularity

**Observed:** the supplied six-poster crop uses broad horizontal transitions among blue, cream, orange, dark gray and ochre, with condensed information and looser script. The restrained layouts matter as much as the color. At this screenshot resolution, softness and muted mixing are visible; the precise grain-production method is not verifiable.

**Working description:** “dirty” can mean intentional noise, uneven density, desaturation, unexpected intermediate colors, imperfect boundaries or a print-like finish. Those are different choices. Adding random noise to a normal gradient does not by itself produce an authored identity.

**Documented adjacent case:** [Pentagram's Intrinsic](https://www.pentagram.com/work/intrinsic) uses gradient echoes and particles to represent possible future robot positions. A generative tool provides behavioral parameters tied to the software; restrained technical typography and flat color provide a counterweight. This is not documented as a “Dirty Gradients” project, but it demonstrates how gradient behavior can explain something.

**Interpretation:** a field can suggest dawn, heat, depth or energy, while irregularity prevents a mathematically pristine surface from dominating the mood. Meaning depends on adjacent content: the same amber haze may read as sunset beside travel imagery and as temperature beside an engineering diagram. Legibility must be evaluated across the actual varying background, including mobile crops. A grain treatment may also disappear or become harsh after compression; the export requires inspection.

## 4. Spray: gesture, authorship and context

**Observed:** the supplied white coffee package uses vivid blue loose lettering, mountain/animal-like drawings and visibly soft sprayed edges. Small black product information provides a separate reading layer. The large marks feel immediate because they preserve the variability of a gesture, not because the entire package lacks order.

**Documented contextual counterexample:** [Pentagram's American Weed Co.](https://www.pentagram.com/work/american-weed-co) draws on military markings, including spray-painted stencils, after discovery with founders and veterans. This is a different use of spray vocabulary from the supplied playful coffee package. It demonstrates that production resemblance alone does not establish cultural meaning.

**Interpretation:** spray can suggest informal authorship, movement, street culture, workshop markings or protest, depending on the lettering, palette, imagery and product. It can make an otherwise precise layout feel alive. But genuine gesture is a designed asset, not permission to make critical text hard to read. One useful combination is expressive marks for emphasis with precise typesetting for facts. Avoid assuming the coffee package's original artist from a repost account or readable product name.

## 5. Holographic Iridescence: light-dependent material

**Observed:** the overview's small tile shows metallic-looking gray forms with shifting pastel/rainbow highlights. It is sufficient to discuss a visual impression, not identify the object, maker or physical process. “Holographic” is often used loosely in aesthetic conversation; a multicolor sheen is not evidence of an actual hologram.

**Documented manufacturer reference:** [3M's Dichroic DF Chill](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/v000096543/) changes apparent color with viewing angle, and has different transmitted and reflected color ranges. This offers a real material reference for thinking about a simulated surface rather than merely placing a rainbow gradient everywhere.

**Interpretation:** carefully controlled highlights can communicate a responsive, unusual or precious material. Broad saturated rainbow sheen can instead suggest novelty packaging or club culture. Geometry, lighting direction, background and scale determine the effect together. A digital imitation should maintain a coherent light/material story; a flat multicolor fill and a reflective object do different jobs. Motion is optional: a well-chosen still can communicate the material without constant animation.

## Combining styles by assigning jobs

An effective exploratory system can assign **structure** to typography/grid, **material** to one family of objects, **color** to a controlled gradient vocabulary, and **detail** to a limited gesture or finish. This is a design heuristic, not a universal formula. For example, organic geometry with one iridescent material may be coherent; combining clay softness, chrome, fur, spray, and multiple gradient directions on every component is harder to read because each element demands attention.

[Play Studio's Adobe MAX case](https://play.studio/work/adobe-max) is a useful primary example of controlled variety. Its account describes the Adobe lens as an organizing device across imagery and motion, with core brand colors and varied secondary artwork. It also openly describes human-directed collage with generative tools. The transferable lesson is to inspect the organizing decisions and specificity of the work, rather than infer quality or provenance from an effect alone.

## What the evidence cannot prove

These sources establish authorship, design intent and mechanisms; they do not establish a universal style ranking. A popular reel selects memorable pictures under conditions different from finding information or booking a service. Studio case studies are also curated accounts; favorable client comments cannot isolate the effect of one visual technique. A design may be famous because of the underlying brand, distribution or novelty.

Useful next research questions are therefore concrete: what should a visitor understand, which element creates that understanding, how does it behave at a small size, and what remains when motion or decoration is absent? Comparable specimens and audience comprehension checks can investigate those questions. No Levarum logo, palette, typography or website implementation is approved by this research.
